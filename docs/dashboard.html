Oral Defense FAQ
Q1: If payloads are encrypted, how does the model infer the domain?
Cryptography encrypts payload text, but packet lengths, directional burst counts, and timing cross the wire in cleartext. Because different domains yield distinct DNS answer record sizes and CNAME chains, their volumetric flow silhouettes remain identifiable.
Q2: Why did deep sequence models (1D-CNN / GRU) achieve only 1.0%–1.4% accuracy?
Encrypted DNS exchanges are compact micro-flows (8–15 packets). They lack translational sequential depth. Discriminative signal resides in aggregate volumetric envelope features (downstream bytes, burst ratios) where decision trees excel.
Q3: Why does Google Public DNS show zero privacy improvement under DoH/3?
Google returns unpadded wire-format answers (RFC 8467). Domain-specific answer sizes pass directly into transport streams, proving resolver implementation overrides transport protocols.
